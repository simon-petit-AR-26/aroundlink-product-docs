# Agenda

The agenda brings **every date of your institution into one place**: semesters, campaigns,
your partners' deadlines, onboarding steps, nominations and events.

These dates used to live in five different screens, and nobody could simply answer "what is
due next month?".

## What you find there

**What it's for.** Seeing what is coming. A nomination deadline, a campaign closing and a
partner's term start are not the same kind of thing, but they fall in the same month, and
that is what matters when you organise your work.

**Who it's for.** <span class="al-audience">the whole mobility team</span>

**How it works.** The agenda **invents no date and changes none**: it assembles what already
exists elsewhere. Each entry links back to the object it comes from, and that is where the
date is edited — otherwise two versions of the same deadline would end up contradicting
each other.

You switch between a **list view** and a **month view**, and you filter on:

- **the family** — Semesters, Campaigns, Partners, Nomination, Onboarding, Application,
  Other;
- **the direction** — outgoing or incoming;
- **your team only**, to set aside what does not concern you.

Filters stack, and the filter bar stays in view as the list scrolls.

You can also **add a date** by hand, for what exists nowhere else: a committee meeting, an
internal deadline.

## Finding your dates in Outlook

**What it's for.** Reading AroundLink's dates from the calendar you already use, without
opening the tool.

**Who it's for.** <span class="al-audience">the whole mobility team, and your students</span>

**How it works.** AroundLink publishes your agenda in the standard format every calendar has
been able to read for twenty years. You copy the subscription address, paste it into
Outlook, and your dates appear — updating by themselves from then on.

This is not a Microsoft integration: it is an open format. **Outlook, Google Calendar, Apple
Calendar and the rest are served the same way**, and your IT department has nothing to
authorise or configure.

!!! info "Synchronisation only goes one way"
    Your calendar comes to **read** AroundLink's dates. It does not change them: a
    nomination deadline should not be movable from a student's Outlook. To change a date, go
    through the screen it comes from.

**You subscribe to what you are looking at.** The address carries the filters displayed when
you copy it. So you can create several subscriptions — "my nominations" on one side, "the
team's events" on the other — and see them as two separate calendars in Outlook, shown or
hidden independently.

**What the feed contains.** Dates and their titles, nothing else. No student name and no
personal data travel through this channel.

**Use case.**
> The coordinator subscribes to their team's agenda filtered on outgoing students. Every
> Monday they see the week's deadlines in Outlook without opening AroundLink. When a date
> changes in the tool, their calendar follows.

!!! warning "The subscription address is personal"
    It gives access to your agenda, and only yours. Do not pass it on: anyone you gave it to
    would see your dates. Each person creates their own from their own account.
