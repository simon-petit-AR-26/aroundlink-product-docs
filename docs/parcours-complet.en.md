# A mobility from end to end

This page follows **one whole mobility**, from the first setting to the transcript, saying at
each step **who acts** and **what triggers the next one**.

The rest of the documentation describes screens one by one. This one describes the sequence —
which is often what is missing when you are looking for why a file is not moving.

## Before any campaign: the groundwork

Nothing below works if these four things do not exist.

| What you need | Who prepares it | Without it |
|---|---|---|
| **Your partners** and their **agreements** | Coordinator | No destination to offer |
| **Places** set in the matrix | Coordinator | Destinations exist but open nothing |
| **Your tracks** (one per cohort, or one per level) | Administrator, in settings | The matching round refuses to start |
| **Your mobility periods** | Administrator | Places have no column to sit in |

!!! tip "The most common false start"
    Creating a campaign before setting any places. The campaign opens, students see it, and
    find no destination. Check the matrix before you open.

## 1. The campaign opens

**Who acts:** the coordinator.

They create the campaign, choose **who takes part** and **where one can go**, set the number
of wishes, the required documents and the few options that cannot be revisited later. See
[Setting up a campaign, setting by setting](etablissement/campagnes.md).

The campaign moves from **Draft** to **Ready**, then **Opened**.

**What triggers the next step:** only the **Opened** state accepts wishes. A "Ready" campaign
can be looked at, not filled in.

## 2. The student places their wishes

**Who acts:** the student.

They browse destinations, set some aside as favourites, and place their wishes from the very
screen where they discover them. They rank them by preference.

If you set a **minimum number of wishes**, they cannot submit until they reach it. If you
enabled the "I do not wish to go" answer, they can also declare it — without having to
justify themselves.

**What triggers the next step:** the campaign closing, on its end date or by your hand.
Wishes still in draft then become real wishes.

## 3. Placement

**Who acts:** the coordinator.

Two paths, which coexist.

**The automatic round** serves students in ranking order: the first-ranked gets their best
available wish, and what is left goes to the next. It requires a profile, a track and an
imported ranking from every participant — see
[What matching requires](etablissement/campagnes.md). It can be re-run as often as needed.

**Placing by hand** is constrained by nothing. You seat whoever you want, wherever you want,
ranked or not.

**What triggers the next step:** a placement exists.

## 4. The student accepts — or not

**Who acts:** the student, unless you chose direct assignment.

They receive their proposal and accept or decline it. If you set the campaign to **direct
assignment**, this step disappears: your decision creates the placement.

**What triggers the next step:** acceptance.

## 5. Nominating to the partner

**Who acts:** the coordinator, then the host institution.

You nominate the student to their destination. The partner receives the information and
answers — through the platform, the EWP network, email or a phone call. You record their
answer, stating which channel it came through.

While they have not answered, you can **remind** them (once every twenty-four hours) or
**withdraw** the nomination, which makes the student available for another destination.

A refusal always comes with a reason, passed on to the institution that nominated.

**What triggers the next step:** the partner's confirmation.

## 6. The file fills up

**Who acts:** the student uploads, the coordinator validates.

Contractual documents, language scores, learning agreement. Every expected item is listed,
and the student sees what is missing. You remind the stragglers from the selection bar of
your list, without keeping a separate list.

Depending on the campaign setting, an incomplete file either **blocks** the placement or is
merely **flagged**.

**What triggers the next step:** the file is complete, and the dates are near.

## 7. Departure, then the stay

**Who acts:** everyone, a little at a time.

The student leaves. Their learning agreement can be changed along the way — any component
change asks for a reason. The period's deadlines appear in the
[agenda](etablissement/agenda.md), and in whichever calendar each person subscribed to.

## 8. Coming back

**Who acts:** the host institution, then the student.

The institution that hosted enters the grades in its own scale and issues the **transcript**;
the student is notified and finds it on their file.

The student submits their **feedback** on the host institution — and may allow their photos
to be published. Those who have not answered are reminded like documents.

**What closes the loop:** feedback feeds the partner's record, which the next students will
read before placing their wishes.
