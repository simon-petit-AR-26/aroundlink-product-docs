    Mobility grants¶

    Mobility grants handle the Erasmus+ financial side — and that of your other funding: your grant programmes, awarding students, the documents each payment depends on, the payment circuit through to the accountant, and the file the Commission's Beneficiary Module expects.

    Grant programmes¶

    What it's for. Keep each source of funding apart: this year's Erasmus+ agreement, a regional grant, a campus scheme. Each has its own budget, rates and files.

    Who it's for. grants office / IRO coordinator

    How it works. Bourse opens the list of your programmes; New program creates one: name, academic year, type (Erasmus+ KA131 or Other (regional, campus…)), grant agreement number, start and end dates, and status:

    Status What it allows
    Draft the programme is being set up
    Open it accepts grants
    Closed no new grants

    A student has at most one grant per programme. The year belongs to the programme: it is not picked on the screen.

    Use case.

    The grants office creates "Erasmus+ KA131 2026-2027" with its agreement number, leaves it in Draft while entering its rates, then opens it.

    A programme's screen¶

    What it's for. See where the money and each file stand, on one screen.

    Who it's for. grants office / IRO coordinator

    How it works. At the top, four figures: Agency allocation, Engaged (signed GA), Paid (advances + balances) and Docs pending. If the programme is split into pockets, the Budget by category table gives each one's planned, committed, paid and remaining amounts.

    The Pipeline bar counts files by status and the oldest wait; a click filters the list. Files read as Kanban or Table:

    Column Statuses it groups
    Before award To award, Zero grant, Waiting list
    Contract Contract to generate, Contract sent, Contract signed
    Advance Advance to pay, Advance paid
    In mobility In mobility
    Final documents Final documents expected, Certificate received, Balance to pay, Balance paid
    Closed Closed

    Panels point out what needs action: overpaid files, files waiting on end-of-mobility documents (Remind chases the students), and documents no longer needed that can be deleted.

    Use case.

    In September, the head of office opens her programme: the Pipeline bar shows 12 files in "Advance to pay", the oldest waiting for 9 days. One click and the list shows just those.

    Awarding grants¶

    What it's for. Create files from the mobilities already recorded, and set amounts without working them out by hand.

    Who it's for. grants office

    How it works.

    • Add students offers students with an active assignment to a country that has a rate — destination, period, suggested type —; those who already have a grant are greyed out. Add N creates the files.
    • New grant creates a single file. Next to the amount, Suggest works out a proposal and breaks it down: stay, travel, traineeship, inclusion and green-travel top-ups, total, then advance and balance.
    • In Table view, ticking files opens a decision bar: an amount (or the scale), then Award, Zero grant or Hold for the whole selection.
    • Import CSV loads grants in bulk, with a preview flagging each row in error; an empty amount cell gets the suggested amount.

    Budget safeguards. Creating a grant, importing or awarding in bulk refuses anything that would exceed the year's allocation — "This grant would exceed the remaining allocation for the year by X €" — or the pocket concerned. With no allocation entered, the screen says so instead of letting it through.

    Use case.

    The head of office adds the 48 students placed this year in one go, ticks those going to Germany and clicks Award with the scale: 14 files move to "Contract to generate".

    A grant file¶

    What it's for. Everything about a student's grant, from award to balance.

    Who it's for. grants office

    How it works. The file holds the financial details, bank details, documents and six dated milestones: Grant Agreement signed, Learning Agreement signed, Arrival confirmed, Certificate of attendance, Transcript of Records, EU Survey received.

    The status follows from those milestones and the payments, in order: a file only reaches Closed once the balance is paid and the final documents are in.

    Payment conditions. Depending on your settings, each document can block nothing, block the advance or block the balance. The file names what is missing for each payment; overriding requires a reason. A recorded payment can no longer be rewritten: to correct it, you recalculate the grant.

    A file can be deleted as long as nothing has been paid; after that it is cancelled (reason required) and can be reopened. Its history says who changed what, and when.

    Use case.

    A student's advance stays blocked: the file shows the Learning Agreement is not signed. It is signed the next day, and the advance becomes payable.

    Rates and rules¶

    What it's for. Set the programme's calculation and payment rules once and for all.

    Who it's for. grants office

    How it works. Rates opens the programme's settings: budget and pockets, funded duration, stay rate, travel scale, top-ups, advance percentage, required documents, payment blocks — with what differs from the Commission's default —, reminders (first reminder after the end of mobility, then every N days) and closing the programme.

    Use case.

    For its regional grant, the school sets the advance at 70% and decides the transcript blocks nothing.

    Paying: requests, approval, accounting¶

    What it's for. Move money through three separate hands: who prepares, who approves, who transfers.

    Who it's for. grants office · approver · accountant

    How it works.

    1. Prepare. Payments ▸ New payment request: advance or balance, then the files to pay. Those that cannot be paid say why (blocked, already in a request, file cancelled). Submit for approval sends the request.
    2. Approve. Whoever holds the Payment approval function sees it under Awaiting your approval, then Approve or Send back with a reason.
    3. Transfer. The accountant opens Payments, a section of their own under Institution: approved batches to transfer, with beneficiary, IBAN, BIC, reference and amount. Mark as paid records the transfer, Attach receipt adds the proof. They see no student file.
    Request status
    In preparation being put together
    Submitted awaiting approval
    Returned for correction sent back with a reason
    Approved to transfer
    Paid transferred

    The student is told when a payment is recorded, and confirms they received it — or reports a discrepancy, which shows up under Reported by students.

    Use case.

    The officer prepares October's advances request, the director approves it, the accountant transfers and marks it paid: the 14 files move to "Advance paid".

    The Commission's Beneficiary Module¶

    What it's for. Produce the file the Beneficiary Module expects without rebuilding it by hand, and check the two stay in agreement.

    Who it's for. grants office

    How it works. Erasmus & mail merge opens the export screen:

    • Erasmus file — for the whole programme or a selection, in the Beneficiary Module's exact template. A check runs first: rows that would be rejected are held back, and a blocking issue prevents the download.
    • Mail merge file — the mail-merge file for grant agreements; exported files move to "Contract sent".
    • Export history — which file, by whom, when.

    Re-import BM file links your files to the Beneficiary Module's activity IDs, and Compare with BM shows the differences, read-only, with Download the differences. Export, on the programme screen, only gives the list on screen.

    Use case.

    Before the interim report, the head of office compares her programme with the Beneficiary Module: two amounts differ, she fixes them and exports the Erasmus file.

    On the student side¶

    The Bourse tab of their profile asks whether they are applying for an Erasmus+ grant (saying no does not take them out of Erasmus+), shows their grant and has them confirm each payment received. See the student portal.
