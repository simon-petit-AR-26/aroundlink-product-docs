    Mobility tracking¶

    Once the path is plugged in, tracking answers the only question you ask when you open a file: where does this student stand, and do I have anything to do?

    The same tracking serves both directions — the students you send and the ones you host — with the same words, the same views and the same filters.

    The three phases of a mobility¶

    What it is for. Sorting files in the order of time rather than in the order of steps. You do not steer someone who has not left the way you steer someone who is back.

    Who it is for. the whole mobility team

    How it works. Each step of a path declares its phase, and a file's phase is the first one that is not finished. It is therefore not declarative: it follows from the work actually done.

    Phase Read on a file What it covers
    Before the mobility Not departed everything before departure: documents, learning agreement, insurance
    During the mobility On site the stay: arrival confirmed, courses, on-site follow-up
    After the mobility Returned the return: departure certificate, transcript, feedback
    Closed Closed the file is closed

    A phase follows, it is not typed in

    You do not move a student from one phase to another by hand. Move the steps on — or let the mirrors tick — and the phase follows. A finished file says so even without a closing date.

    Two views of the same tracking¶

    What it is for. The table to work through, the kanban to see what is coming.

    How it works. Both views share the same toolbar: search, sorting, filters, column picker, saved views and export.

    The table lines the files up in rows, with progress in figures, the current step and who is expected. Eight optional columns can be added and sorted: Destination, City, Country, Region, Period, Level, Field of study, Agreement, Workflow.

    The kanban gives one column per phase and one card per student. Each column scrolls inside itself, its title stays visible, and the gauge at the top says how many files have passed the step — mirrors included. You pick up to thirteen facts to show on the cards, and the whole card leads to the file.

    The kanban's search suggests filters as you type: Destination, City, Country, Region, Period, Level, Field of study, Agreement, Phase.

    The export takes what you have in front of you

    The Export what is on screen button takes your filters, your visible columns and your sorting. It is available on both views and in both directions.

    Where to find it¶

    How it works. Tracking appears in four places, and it is always the same screen:

    • a campaign's Mobility tab — every outgoing student of that campaign. If no path is plugged in, the tab says what is missing and offers to plug it in on the spot;
    • a welcome session — every incoming student of that session;
    • a student's Mobility tab — one block per mobility: a student who left twice has two files, each with its dates and its progress;
    • the dashboard, which gives the summary by phase.

    A mobility header always reads the same way: where, what, when — the partner with its logo, the agreement family, the period. And the breadcrumb goes all the way down to the open session.

    The mobility timeline¶

    What it is for. Reading a mobility in time rather than as a list: from the first wish to the closing.

    How it works. The timeline carries real dates and distinguishes its two sources — what comes from the campaign and what comes from the path. It does not go back in time, and the current day does not count as past. Campaign dates appear even if the student has submitted no wish.

    It is also what a student's agenda shows: the dated timeline of their mobility, sorted into overdue, upcoming and past.

    Who is expected¶

    How it works. On every row and every card, Waiting on says who holds the ball: the student, you, or nobody when the step is a mirror. A step that nothing blocks is due, not pending — the distinction saves you looking for a blockage that does not exist.

    Shortcuts lead straight to the object named: a student's name opens their fiche, a path's name opens its workshop, a transcript's badge opens the entry screen.

    Use case.

    At the end of September, the coordinator opens the campaign's Mobility tab. The kanban shows four files still Not departed although classes have started. She filters on Waiting on: you, finds three learning agreements waiting for her signature, handles them, and the three cards move to On site on their own.
